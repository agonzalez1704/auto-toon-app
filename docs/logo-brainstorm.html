AutoToon · Logo Brainstorm · 2026

Shape-shift. Emerge. Belong on a phone screen.

We want a mark that says "your photo becomes something better" — without literally drawing a camera. Two metaphors keep coming up: chameleon (adapts, changes color to context) and butterfly (cocoon → emergence, beautiful transformation). This doc pressure-tests both, plus a few lateral options, against the constraints a real iOS / Android app icon has to survive.

1. Constraints — what the mark has to do

C1

Readable at 40 × 40

Home-screen icon, tab-bar icon, share-sheet icon — all sub-100px. Detail dies fast. Silhouette must work first.

C2

Single-color stencil legibility

Must hold up in monochrome (favicon, watermark, embroidery). If it only reads with color, it's chrome — not a mark.

C3

Pairs with amber CTA

App primary is amber #FBBF24. Logo color stack must not fight it. Violet brand secondary #A78BFA stays for the mark, amber for product.

C4

Owns one idea, not three

"Camera + butterfly + AI sparkle" = nothing. Pick one metaphor, execute it 10× cleaner than the field.

C5

Distinct from Adobe / Canva / Figma

Avoid generic morphing-shapes-with-gradient. Specific motif beats safe abstraction.

2. Butterfly direction · transformation, emergence

Strongest narrative tie: cocoon (boring product photo) → butterfly (polished campaign image). Risk: butterfly is over-used in beauty / wellness. Differentiator = aperture / lens / photo-frame integration.

CONCEPT B1

Aperture Wings

Butterfly where wings are aperture petals. Body is a tiny camera iris. Reads as butterfly AND lens — same shape doing two jobs.

butterfly distinctive scales moderate
CONCEPT B2

Cocoon → Butterfly

Literal before/after pair. Storytelling-heavy. Strong on landing pages, weaker at icon size — cocoon detail disappears below ~64px.

butterfly narrative poor at 40px moderate
CONCEPT B3

Pixel Wing

Butterfly assembled from photo pixels — speaks "made of photos." Maps directly to the product (image transformation). Geometric, scales aggressively well.

butterfly on-message monochrome works low
CONCEPT B4

Liquid Butterfly

Organic, fluid wings — half amber, half violet, meeting at a bright pixel. Soft, premium, mobile-native (matches iOS/visionOS liquid shapes).

butterfly premium feel brand color split moderate
CONCEPT B5

A-Wing Monogram

Two wings collapse into the letter A (for "AutoToon"). Antenna dots hint at butterfly. Sneaks the wordmark into the icon — appears on share previews, watermarks naturally.

butterfly monogram bonus scales tiny low
CONCEPT B6

Photo-Frame Wings

Wings = two polaroid frames tilted into a butterfly silhouette. Story: every shot becomes both a moment AND a beautiful object.

butterfly on-message busy at icon size moderate

3. Chameleon direction · adaptability, color-shift

Chameleon's story: same model, different scene. Maps perfectly to fashion-editorial swap (same model wears 8 outfits in 8 settings). Risk: chameleons are detailed animals — silhouettes can look clinical or like a generic lizard. Best path = stylized geometry, not realism.

CONCEPT C1

Color-Shift Chameleon

Stylized chameleon body running a full color-shift gradient (amber → cyan → violet). Tail-curl is the loudest gesture. Speaks adaptability instantly.

chameleon narrative match complex silhouette moderate
CONCEPT C2

Tail-Curl Glyph

Most iconic part of a chameleon — the spiral tail — as a single stroke. One color, monochrome-perfect. Reads as "loop / transform / return." Could double as a question mark.

chameleon single-stroke tiny-size hero low
CONCEPT C3

Independent Eye

Chameleon's other iconic feature — the eye that rotates 360°. Reads as eye AND camera lens AND vision. Strong dual-meaning.

chameleon dual-meaning feels editorial-magazine-y low
CONCEPT C4

Polygon Chameleon

Body broken into facets, each a different color — "every angle, a different look." Reads chameleon clearly while looking modern/geometric.

chameleon modern look on-message moderate
CONCEPT C5

A-Cham Monogram

Letter A whose right serif extends into a chameleon tail-curl. Combines wordmark + animal in one glyph. Reads at every size, owns "A".

chameleon monogram bonus single color low
CONCEPT C6

Pixel Chameleon

Same "made of pixels" treatment, applied to chameleon. Color-shift built in (the pixels grade across his body).

chameleon on-message busy at small sizes moderate

4. Lateral options · same metaphor, fresher animal

If butterfly + chameleon both feel done-to-death after sketching, two adjacent metaphors carry the same "transformation" payload:

CONCEPT L1

Origami Crane

Flat paper folded into something beautiful — exact metaphor for AI image generation. Owns "transformation through folding." Geometric, low detail, scales infinitely.

lateral tech-craft tone distinct low
CONCEPT L2

Phoenix Spark

Rebirth metaphor — boring photo "burned" and emerges polished. Stronger emotional pull than butterfly but feels heavier / mythic; may overshoot premium aspirational.

lateral emotional overwrought moderate
CONCEPT L3

Shutter Bloom

Camera aperture petals blooming open into a flower — pure photography metaphor without literal camera. Very strong as static icon, less so as a verb.

lateral on-craft no transformation narrative low

7. Recommendation

Pursue two for prototyping. Kill the other ten.

  • B4 Liquid Butterfly — premium feel, mobile-native, brand color split built-in. Strong cocoon→butterfly narrative without literal cocoon.
  • C2 Tail-Curl Glyph — single-stroke, monochrome-perfect, ownable. Reads as "loop / transform / return." Lives at 16px no problem.

They sit on opposite poles of the brief: B4 = ornate / emotional / illustration. C2 = minimal / system / glyph. Prototype both as actual app icons, install on phone, live with them for a week. The one that fades into the home screen the LEAST after 7 days wins.

8. What I need to move forward

  • Vote 1-2 concepts to develop. Decimate the field.
  • Tone preference — illustration (B-series) or geometric glyph (C-series, L-series)?
  • Animal vs symbol — comfortable with a literal animal, or prefer abstract metaphor?
  • Wordmark direction — sit with one of the three type pairings.
